Visual Routine Builder

Make a picture routine for mornings, after school, homework, bedtime, appointments or changing activity, then print it.
FASD Compass · fasdcompass.co.uk/routine-builder/
Picture routines help many children know what comes next, without needing to remember every step or wait to be told. Choose a routine, change the steps and pictures to fit your child, then print it. Keep it where the routine happens, such as by the front door or in the bathroom.

🔒 Your routines stay on this device. Nothing is sent to FASD Compass or anyone else. If you choose Save, the routine is kept in this browser only.

2. Change the steps

Type your own words, choose a picture, and use the arrows to change the order. Keep each step short. Most children manage best with 4 to 8 steps.

    Using Your Routine

    📍Put it where it happens
    Stick the routine up where it is used, at your child’s eye level. A bedtime routine belongs in the bedroom or bathroom, and a leaving-the-house routine by the front door.
    🤝Go through it together
    At first, do the routine together, pointing to each picture. Praise each step. Over time, step back and let the pictures do the reminding.
    🔁Keep it the same
    Use the same order every day, including weekends where you can. When something changes, change the routine and show your child before the day it happens.
    🎯Every child is different
    Some children like lots of steps. Others manage better with two or three. Use real photos instead of pictures if that works better for your child. A now and next board can help with single changes.
    📚 Visual routines and warnings before changes are among the strategies suggested in National FASD parenting tips. For more ideas, see the Everyday Challenges Library.
