What Does This Behaviour Mean?

A classroom guide to common difficulties, what might be behind them, and what helps.
FASD Compass · fasdcompass.co.uk/behaviour-guide/
Behaviour is often a way of showing that something is hard. Choose a common classroom difficulty to see what might be behind it and what helps. The ideas work for many pupils, whether or not they have FASD or any diagnosis.
⚖️
No behaviour is diagnostic of FASD
The difficulties below are common in many children. They can be linked to language difficulties, learning needs, ADHD, autism, anxiety, trauma, sleep problems, hearing or vision problems, illness, bullying or events at home. This guide does not identify, confirm or rule out FASD. Never guess about alcohol exposure in pregnancy from a pupil’s behaviour, family or care history.
🛡️
If you are worried a child is being harmed
Follow your school’s safeguarding or child protection procedures straight away and speak to your designated safeguarding lead. See SEN support and safeguarding.

Choose a Difficulty

  • Does not follow instructions
  • Forgets what was learned
  • Seems not to listen
  • Leaves their seat or wanders
  • Refuses or avoids work
  • Outbursts or meltdowns
  • Says things that are not true
  • Takes things that belong to others
  • Struggles with transitions
  • Social conflict or friendship difficulties
  • Finds break, lunch or trips hard
  • Homework is not done
  • Performance varies from day to day

Does not follow instructions

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Has not understood the words used, even if they can repeat them
  • Has lost track of a long or multi-step instruction
  • Needs more time to process what was said
  • Did not hear it, because of noise, distraction or a hearing difficulty
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Give one instruction at a time, in the order it should be done
  • Show as well as tell, by modelling the first step
  • Leave a written or picture checklist on the desk
  • Check understanding by asking the pupil to show you
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Reduce background noise when giving instructions
  • Seat the pupil where they can see and hear you clearly
  • Keep a visual timetable and task board in sight
💬Communication strategies
  • Say the pupil’s name and get their attention first
  • Use short, plain sentences
  • Wait before repeating, then use the same words
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • There are concerns about hearing, vision or understanding of language
  • Difficulties continue despite consistent support over time

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Forgets what was learned

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Difficulty storing or finding information in memory
  • Learning was taught once, without enough practice
  • Tiredness, stress or a poor night
  • The skill was taught in a different setting
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Expect to reteach, and plan regular recaps
  • Use the same routines, words and visuals each time
  • Practise skills in different places and contexts
  • Use hands-on and multi-sensory teaching
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Display key facts and vocabulary where they are used
  • Use memory aids, such as checklists, number lines and word banks
💬Communication strategies
  • Avoid saying “but you knew this yesterday”
  • Praise effort and small steps
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • Progress is much slower than expected despite good teaching
  • There are big differences between what the pupil can do on different days or in different subjects

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Seems not to listen

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Attention is easily pulled away by other things
  • Has switched off because the language is too complex
  • Is anxious, tired or overwhelmed
  • May have a hearing difficulty
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Break talk into short chunks with pauses
  • Use visuals and demonstrations alongside speech
  • Build in chances to move and respond
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Seat near the teacher and away from doors and windows
  • Reduce clutter and noise around the pupil
💬Communication strategies
  • Get the pupil’s attention before speaking
  • Ask them to show you what they need to do
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • A hearing check has not been done recently
  • Attention difficulties affect learning in most lessons and at home

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Leaves their seat or wanders

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Needs to move to stay alert
  • Is avoiding a task that feels too hard
  • Is overwhelmed by noise or a busy room
  • Is unsure what to do next
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Plan short movement breaks or jobs into lessons
  • Break tasks into small steps with a clear finish
  • Use a visual timer so the pupil can see how long is left
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Agree a place the pupil can go, and how they ask
  • Offer a standing desk or wobble cushion if suitable
💬Communication strategies
  • Use calm, private reminders
  • Praise the pupil when they return or stay with the task
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • The pupil leaves the room or the school site, or puts themselves in danger
  • Restlessness is constant across settings

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Refuses or avoids work

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • The task feels too hard or unclear
  • Fear of getting it wrong or being seen to fail
  • Tiredness or sensory overload
  • Does not know how to start
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Make the first step very small and easy to succeed at
  • Offer a choice between two tasks or ways to work
  • Show a finished example
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Reduce the amount on the page
  • Offer a quieter place to work
💬Communication strategies
  • Avoid public confrontation
  • Use “first, then” language, such as first maths, then computer
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • Refusal is increasing or spreading to school attendance
  • The pupil appears very anxious, low in mood or withdrawn

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Outbursts or meltdowns

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Overwhelmed by noise, demands or change
  • Has misunderstood what happened or what was said
  • Hungry, tired, in pain or unwell
  • Something has happened at home or with peers
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Learn the early signs and act before things escalate
  • Teach calming strategies when the pupil is calm, not during an outburst
  • Plan for known hard times of the day
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Agree a calm space and how the pupil gets there
  • Lower the noise and remove an audience
💬Communication strategies
  • Use few words and a calm, low voice
  • Talk about what happened later, when the pupil is calm, using pictures or drawing
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • Outbursts are frequent, severe or put anyone at risk
  • There has been a change in behaviour that has no clear cause

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Says things that are not true

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Is filling gaps in memory with what seems to make sense (sometimes called confabulation)
  • Wants to please the adult or give the answer they think is wanted
  • Is easily influenced by leading questions
  • Is trying to avoid getting into trouble
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Focus on what needs to happen now, rather than proving what happened
  • Use visual records, such as photos or a diary, to support memory
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Have more than one adult or source of information when something important happens
💬Communication strategies
  • Ask open questions, such as “tell me what happened”, not leading ones
  • Avoid asking the pupil to promise or explain why
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • The pupil describes something that could mean they are being harmed. Follow your safeguarding procedures straight away.

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Takes things that belong to others

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Does not fully understand ownership, or forgets whose it is
  • Acts on impulse before thinking
  • Is being encouraged by peers
  • Needs something, such as food or equipment
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Teach ownership rules directly and practise them
  • Label belongings and shared equipment clearly
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Reduce chances, by keeping valuable items out of sight
  • Supervise at times when this happens most
💬Communication strategies
  • Calmly return the item without a long conversation
  • Use simple, concrete rules, such as “ask before you touch”
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • The pupil may not have enough food, clothing or equipment at home. Follow your safeguarding procedures.
  • Peers may be exploiting the pupil

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Struggles with transitions

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Change feels unpredictable and unsafe
  • Finds it hard to stop one activity and start another
  • Is unsettled by new adults, rooms or routines
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Give warnings before changes, with a visual timer
  • Use a now and next board
  • Prepare the pupil for planned changes, such as trips or supply staff
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Keep the visual timetable up to date and in sight
  • Offer a buddy or early leave pass for moving around school
💬Communication strategies
  • Explain changes simply and early
  • Use the same words for the same routines
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • Transitions lead to distress most days
  • Anxiety is affecting attendance or wellbeing

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Social conflict or friendship difficulties

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Finds it hard to read social cues or understand others’ intentions
  • Social skills may be younger than their age
  • Is easily led or wants to fit in
  • May be being bullied
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Teach social skills directly, using role play and stories
  • Use structured group roles in lessons
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Offer structured activities or a club at break and lunch
  • Make sure supervising staff know the pupil’s plan
💬Communication strategies
  • Talk through situations afterwards, using drawings or comic strips
  • Avoid assuming intent to hurt others
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • The pupil is often isolated, bullied or targeted
  • There are concerns about exploitation, online risk or unsafe relationships. Follow your safeguarding procedures.

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Finds break, lunch or trips hard

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Unstructured time has no clear rules or routine
  • Busy, noisy spaces cause overload
  • Social demands are high
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Teach what to do at break and lunch, using visuals
  • Plan trips carefully, with clear expectations and a named adult
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Offer a quieter space or club option
  • Increase supervision where incidents happen
💬Communication strategies
  • Remind the pupil of the plan just before break
  • Check in briefly afterwards
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • Incidents at break or lunch happen often or are getting worse

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Homework is not done

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Has forgotten it was set, or what to do
  • Too tired after a demanding school day
  • Home routines are already a struggle
  • Does not understand the task without support
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Write homework down for the pupil or give a printed copy
  • Keep homework short, clear and practice based
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Offer a homework club or time in school
💬Communication strategies
  • Agree homework expectations with the family
  • Avoid removing break as a sanction for missed homework
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • Homework is causing distress at home
  • The pupil cannot complete work without full adult support

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.

Performance varies from day to day

Many children show this behaviour. It has many possible causes and is not a sign of FASD on its own.

🔍Possible underlying needs
  • Memory and attention can vary from day to day
  • Sleep, health, stress and changes affect learning
  • The demands of the task or setting may have changed
🍎Supportive teaching approaches
  • Teach to the pupil’s typical day, not their best day
  • Keep a simple record of good and hard days to spot patterns
🏫Environmental adjustments
  • Keep routines and support the same, even on good days
💬Communication strategies
  • Avoid saying “you could do it yesterday”
  • Share good and hard days with the family
📣When more assessment may be needed
  • The pattern is getting worse or there has been a sudden change
  • There are concerns about sleep, health or something happening outside school

Talk to your SENCO or ALNCo and the family. Ask about hearing and vision checks, and advice from an educational psychologist, speech and language therapist or a health professional, as needed.
