Your FASD Journey

  1. 1✓
    💭Noticing a concernNot started

    What happens

    • You notice your child finds some things much harder than other children of the same age, such as memory, attention, learning, behaviour or friendships.
    • You find out, or suspect, that your child was exposed to alcohol before birth.
    • You start to gather information. You do not need proof before asking for help.

    Questions to ask

    • What have I noticed, and since when?
    • Does the school or nursery see the same things?
    • What do I know about alcohol in the pregnancy, and where does that information come from?

    What you should leave with

    Helpful: Evidence checklist · Concerns guide · Records for adoptive, foster and kinship families

  2. 2✓
    🩺Seeing the GPNot started

    What happens

    • You explain your concerns and what you know about alcohol exposure.
    • NICE QS204 says children with probable prenatal alcohol exposure and significant difficulties should be referred for assessment.
    • The GP refers your child to community paediatrics, a child development centre or a neurodevelopmental team.

    Questions to ask

    • Will you refer my child, and to which service?
    • Will you say in the referral that FASD should be considered?
    • Will you record the alcohol exposure in my child’s records?
    • How long is the wait, and who do I contact if I hear nothing?

    What you should leave with

    Helpful: Guide for GPs · Template letter for your GP

  3. 3✓
    👩‍⚕️Seeing the paediatricianNot started

    What happens

    • This may be a community paediatrician, a child development centre or a neurodevelopmental team.
    • A doctor takes a full history, examines your child and measures growth and head size.
    • They may check for other possible causes of your child’s difficulties. This can include tests such as hearing, vision or genetic tests.
    • They decide whether a full FASD assessment is needed and who will carry it out.

    Questions to ask

    • Will FASD be considered alongside other conditions?
    • Can your service do a full FASD assessment? If not, where will my child be referred?
    • Can you write to the school about my child’s needs while we wait?

    What you should leave with

    Helpful: Template letter for the paediatrician · While You Wait · No FASD Service Near You?

  4. 4✓
    🧩The FASD assessmentNot started

    What happens

    • SIGN 156 says FASD should be assessed by a multidisciplinary team, such as a paediatrician, psychologist and speech and language therapist.
    • The team looks at 10 areas of brain function, including memory, attention, language, learning and daily living skills.
    • It can take several appointments. The school is usually asked for information too.

    Questions to ask

    • Which professionals will see my child?
    • How many appointments will there be, and how long will it take?
    • Will the school be asked for information?
    • Will I get a written report?

    What you should leave with

    Helpful: How FASD is assessed

  5. 5✓
    📄The outcomeNot started

    What happens

    • The team meets you to explain what they found.
    • Under SIGN 156 there are three possible FASD outcomes: FASD with sentinel facial features, FASD without sentinel facial features, or at increased risk of FASD.
    • The assessment may also find a different explanation, or another condition alongside FASD.

    Questions to ask

    • What does the outcome mean for my child?
    • Which areas of brain function are affected, and which are strengths?
    • Will the outcome be coded in my child’s GP records?
    • If my child is at increased risk, when will they be seen again?

    What you should leave with

    Helpful: Explaining FASD to your child

  6. 6✓
    🗂️The management planNot started

    What happens

    • NICE QS204 Statement 5 says every child diagnosed with FASD should have an individualised management plan.
    • It should cover immediate and long-term needs and signpost to services, such as therapy, school support and help for the family.
    • It should be shared with you, the school and other professionals involved.

    Questions to ask

    • Who will coordinate my child’s care?
    • Who will get a copy of the plan?
    • What support will school be asked to give?
    • When will the plan be reviewed?

    What you should leave with

    Helpful: Personalised support plan · School Advocacy Toolkit · EHC needs assessment letter

  7. 7✓
    🌱Ongoing support and reviewNot started

    What happens

    • Needs change as your child grows. Moving school, puberty and becoming an adult are times when extra support may be needed.
    • Keep the plan up to date and share it with anyone new who works with your child.
    • Support should be based on your child’s needs at every stage.

    Questions to ask

    • Is the plan still working?
    • Who needs to know about my child’s FASD at the next stage?
    • What changes at 16 and 18, and who will help with that move?

    What you should leave with

    Helpful: Age-based support pathways · Meltdowns and Big Feelings

If You Get Stuck

⏳The wait is long
Support does not have to wait for a diagnosis. Use While You Wait for help at home and school now, and ask the school for SEN support.
🚧There is no service
Many areas do not yet have an FASD pathway. See No FASD Service Near You? and use the letter to your ICB.
🔁You have heard nothing
Contact the service that holds the referral and ask where your child is on the list. Write down who you spoke to and when, in the notes for that step.
