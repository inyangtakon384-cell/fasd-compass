Before You See the GP

A checklist of what to gather before you ask for an FASD referral, so your GP has what they need.
FASD Compass · fasdcompass.co.uk/gp-checklist/
⬇️ Download PDF

Want to write to your GP first? Use our template letter.

A GP cannot diagnose FASD, but they can refer your child for an assessment. National guidance says a referral should be made when a child has significant difficulties and there is probable alcohol exposure in pregnancy (NICE QS204 Statement 3). This checklist helps you show both.

You do not need everything on this list. Bring what you have. Tick items off as you gather them, then print the page or take it with you on your phone.

1. Alcohol in Pregnancy

📚 This is the most important part. SIGN 156 and NICE QS204 accept these kinds of evidence (SIGN 156, NICE QS204).
🍷What counts as evidence
💬If you have no information
That is common, especially in adoption. Tell the GP what you know and what you do not know. Clear photos of your child's face are still useful, because in a small number of children the facial features alone can allow a diagnosis (National FASD).

5. At the Appointment

🗣️What to say
You could say: "I would like my child referred for an assessment that considers alcohol exposure in pregnancy as a possible cause of their difficulties, as set out in NICE quality standard QS204."

📞More help
The National FASD diagnosis guide explains the process in more detail, and SIGN has a free leaflet for families on FASD assessment. The National FASD helpline is 0208 458 5951.
