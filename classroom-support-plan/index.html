Classroom Support Plan Generator

Build a needs-led support plan for a pupil in about ten minutes, then edit, download and share it.
FASD Compass · fasdcompass.co.uk/classroom-support-plan/
For teachers, SENCOs, ALNCos and support staff. Tick what you see in class, and the tool will suggest practical support for each area. It covers ten areas: strengths, learning and memory, processing and communication, sensory environment, attention, emotional regulation, transitions, assessment adjustments, home-school communication and review arrangements. You can edit every line before you download or print it. Work on the plan with the pupil and their parents or carers where you can.
🧭
Needs first, not labels
This tool does not diagnose or rule out FASD or any other condition. It is not a clinical assessment. Support in school should be based on the pupil’s needs and does not depend on a diagnosis.

🔒 No identifiable information is needed. Use initials or leave the name blank. Do not enter a full name, date of birth or address. Nothing is sent to FASD Compass or anyone else. If you choose Save, the plan stays in this browser only. Follow your school’s data protection policy when you store or share the finished plan.

About the plan
1. Strengths

Start with what the pupil does well. Strengths and interests are the best way in to learning.

2. Learning and memory

Many pupils with FASD learn something one day and seem to lose it the next. This is about how the brain stores and finds information, not effort.

3. Processing and communication

Good speech can hide difficulties with understanding. A pupil may repeat instructions back without being able to follow them.

4. Sensory environment

Some pupils are very sensitive to noise, light, touch or busy spaces. Others seek out movement or do not notice pain, cold or hunger.

5. Attention

Attention can vary across the day and between subjects. Tiredness, hunger and stress all make it harder.

6. Emotional regulation

Big reactions are often a sign that a pupil is overwhelmed, confused or anxious. Calm adults and predictable routines help most.

7. Transitions

Changes, big or small, can be very hard. Planning ahead reduces anxiety and helps the pupil feel safe.

8. Assessment adjustments

Tests may show a pupil’s worst day, not what they can do. For formal exams, follow the access arrangements rules of the exam board.

9. Home-school communication

Parents and carers know the pupil best. Regular, two-way contact helps everyone spot patterns early.

Using the Plan

🤝Share it with every adult
Give a copy to everyone who works with the pupil, including lunchtime staff, cover teachers and club leaders. Add the one-page pupil profile for a quick summary.
🔁Assess, plan, do, review
Try the plan, see what works, and change it. If the pupil is not making progress with good support in place, talk to your SENCO or ALNCo about the next step. See school support pathways for each UK nation.
