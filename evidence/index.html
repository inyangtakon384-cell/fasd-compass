Evidence and Sources

The guidance and research behind FASD Compass, and when we review it.
FASD Compass · fasdcompass.co.uk/evidence/

How we use evidence

FASD Compass is based mainly on UK clinical guidance. Where we use a figure or a recommendation, we try to name the source on the same page. We keep clinical guidance separate from practical tips based on experience.

Main UK guidance

  • SIGN 156: Children and young people exposed prenatally to alcohol. Scottish Intercollegiate Guidelines Network. Published January 2019, revalidated 2022. SIGN currently notes that some recommendations may be out of date.
  • NICE Quality Standard QS204: Fetal alcohol spectrum disorder. National Institute for Health and Care Excellence. Published March 2022. A quality standard for England. Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own arrangements.
  • UK Chief Medical Officers’ low risk drinking guidelines. Department of Health and Social Care, 2016.

Figures used on this site

  • About 3.2% of babies born in the UK are affected by FASD. Estimate quoted by SIGN.
  • Up to 172,000 people in Scotland may be affected. Estimate quoted by SIGN.
  • 1.8% of children had FASD, or 3.6% including possible cases, in three Greater Manchester primary schools. McCarthy R, Mukherjee RAS, Fleming KM, et al. Prevalence of fetal alcohol spectrum disorder in Greater Manchester, UK: an active case ascertainment study. Alcoholism: Clinical and Experimental Research. 2021;45(11):2271-2281. A small study of 220 children aged 8 to 9. The authors say a larger study is needed before the figures can be applied to the whole population.
  • 428 conditions that co-occur with FASD, across 18 of 22 ICD-10 chapters. Popova S, Lange S, Shield K, et al. Comorbidity of fetal alcohol spectrum disorder: a systematic review and meta-analysis. The Lancet. 2016;387(10022):978-987.

Information for families

  • National FASD. UK charity offering information and support.
  • SIGN: information about FASD assessment. Booklet for families.
